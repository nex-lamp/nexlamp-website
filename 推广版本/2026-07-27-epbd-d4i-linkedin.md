EU EPBD 2026 is now in full force — and it just moved the goalposts for lighting exporters.

Starting May 2026, every new commercial building in the EU must have automatic lighting control and a building automation system (BAS). Smart lighting has shifted from "nice-to-have" to "mandatory."

But the real gatekeeper is the LED driver.

To sell into EU new-build commercial projects, luminaires now need D4i Gen 2 certified drivers. Not just DALI dimming — D4i adds:

✅ Energy metering (DALI Parts 250–253)
✅ Diagnostic reporting
✅ Pluggable, independently mounted drivers
✅ No shared thermal path with the LED module

For Chinese SMEs, the smartest path is staged:
1. Swap to D4i-certified drivers — solve the hardware gate first.
2. Add standard connectivity modules (Matter/Zigbee/SRI) — solve "can it connect."
3. Deepen into BAS integration and energy analytics once compliance is stable.

Trying to build the entire protocol stack in-house is expensive and risky. The winners will be the ones who move fast on the driver, then layer intelligence on top.

This is not just an EU story. D4i supply chains will scale, costs will fall, and the same expectations will reach domestic high-end commercial projects within 3–5 years.

The luminaire is no longer a standalone product. It is a node in the building's energy network.
