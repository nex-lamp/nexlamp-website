No-Main-Light Design Done Right: A Professional Guide to Smart Downlight & Spotlight Selection

Search "no-main-light" on Chinese home decor platforms and you'll find over 800,000 posts — and a shocking number of them are failure stories. As a lighting manufacturer working with contractors and smart home integrators, here's what we've learned about getting it right the first time.

The Three-Zone Method

Every successful no-main-light installation follows a simple framework:

1. Ambient Zone — Grid downlights at 1.2-1.5m spacing, 3-4W LED/sqm, 50-70cm from walls
2. Accent Zone — Spotlights (15-24° beam) for feature walls, art, and focal points
3. Mood Zone — Hidden LED strips for indirect ambient light

The most common failure? Downlights placed too close to walls, creating harsh hot spots instead of smooth illumination.

Downlight vs Spotlight: Quick Reference for Specifiers

| Parameter | Downlight | Spotlight |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Beam angle | 90-120° | 15-50° |
| CRI minimum | Ra 90 | Ra 95 (98 recommended) |
| Application | General lighting | Wall washing, accent |
| Anti-glare | UGR < 19 | Deep recessed |

Smart Dimming: The Integration Challenge

The #1 technical issue we see: mixed dimming protocols. Using a TRIAC dimmer with Zigbee smart drivers creates flickering and erratic behavior. The solution is simple — choose one protocol and commit to it.

For reliability at scale, we recommend Tuya Zigbee with smart LED drivers across all fixtures. Mesh networking requires at least 3 router nodes for stable coverage; place a mains-powered Zigbee relay on each floor.

Key takeaway for contractors: always specify neutral wire at every fixture position. No-neutral switches are unreliable with smart lighting loads.
