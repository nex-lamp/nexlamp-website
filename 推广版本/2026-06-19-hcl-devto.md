## How Light Controls Your Body Clock

Your retina contains specialized photoreceptors called **ipRGCs** (intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells). They don't care about what you "see" — they monitor ambient light for **blue wavelengths (~480nm)**.

- **Blue light detected** → melatonin suppressed → brain: "It's daytime, stay alert"
- **Blue light absent** → melatonin released → brain: "Time to sleep"

The problem? Your living room LEDs emit 4000-6500K light — with blue content comparable to noon sunlight. At 10 PM, your body thinks it's 3 PM. Melatonin onset delays by **30–90 minutes**. Over weeks, this accumulates: poor sleep, daytime fatigue, mood dips.

## The Four-Phase Circadian Lighting Schedule

| Time | CCT | Brightness | Effect |
|------|-----|------------|--------|
| 6-8 AM | 5000-6500K | 80-100% | Suppress melatonin, boost cortisol |
| 9 AM - 4 PM | 4000-5000K | 70-100% | Maintain alertness |
| 5-7 PM | 3000-4000K | 50-70% | Begin circadian wind-down |
| 8-10 PM | 2200-2700K | 20-40% | Support melatonin onset |

**Critical mistake**: dimming a 5000K light does NOT make it circadian-friendly. The blue wavelengths remain — you must shift color temperature AND reduce intensity.
